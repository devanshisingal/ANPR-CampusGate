=== [1] 5-MINUTE ELEVATOR DEFENSE SCRIPT (~680 words) ===

"Good morning respected panel members. I am presenting ANPR-CampusGate, an
end-to-end Automatic Number Plate Recognition and multi-gate vehicle tracking
system built specifically for real-world Indian campus gates.

Traditional campus gates rely on slow manual entry registers or basic single-
image OCR tools that choke on motion blur, oblique angles, and ambiguous Indian
characters like zero versus 'O', one versus 'I', or eight versus 'B'. Our
objective was to engineer a reliable, CPU-deployable pipeline that not only
logs entries with IN/OUT direction and vehicle attributes, but also raises an
immediate security alert on watchlisted vehicles before they even clear the gate.

Looking at Slide 5 [Figure 01], the architecture spans five integrated layers.
Video frames enter through FrameCapture and CameraManager, pass into an ONNX
YOLOv8n vehicle detector and supervision ByteTrack tracker, localize license
plates via a 3.14-million-parameter YOLOv8n ONNX detector at 320px, decode text
using CPU-tuned RapidOCR with Indian RTO syntax repair, aggregate reads through
weighted temporal OCRFusion, and persist atomic records in SQLite WAL across two
logically linked tables — 22-column vehicle_events and 25-column
vehicle_profiles — surfaced through a 41-endpoint FastAPI backend to a Next.js
15 operator console.

Three architectural decisions drive our practical performance [Figure 02].
First, decoupling blacklist alerting from fusion consensus: as soon as a
single high-confidence frame matches blacklist.yaml, an alert fires ahead of
database commit — measured at 188 milliseconds to 1,127 milliseconds earlier
across test passes. Second, early track lock-out: once ByteTrack track ID 'tid'
reaches consensus, stored_tracks skips redundant plate YOLO and RapidOCR work
on exit frames. Third, CPU optimization: replacing PyTorch .pt inference and
unbounded DBNet upscaling with quantized ONNX sessions, det_limit_side_len=320,
and disabled angle classification boosted Ryzen 7 CPU throughput over 15x — from
1.25 FPS baseline to 19.18 FPS across a full 1,500-frame benchmark (peaking at
23.76 FPS rolling) while preserving 6/6 vehicle capture, whereas naive
frame_skip=2 dropped a vehicle.

On training and datasets [Figures 04-06], our audit ensures full scientific
honesty. Our YOLOv8s detector train-3 achieved 0.9832 validation mAP50 across
18,578 training and 1,973 validation images, while deployed YOLOv8n ONNX reached
0.9783 peak val mAP50. Just as importantly, our self-audit uncovered that an
earlier legacy YOLOv8m run had val==train data leakage, that documented
'25,674' counts double-counted an imaginary 4,116 validation split, and that a
full labeled campus OCR Character Error Rate benchmark remains future work
since PARSeq ONNX failed on CPU due to Where(16) and RapidOCR was evaluated via
E2E runs rather than a labeled OCR split.

Finally [Figure 08], out of 758 pytest cases across 44 files, 404 non-socket
unit tests, 160 non-socket integration DB/pipeline tests, 25 FastAPI ASGI
tests, and a 375-frame E2E clip smoke test passed cleanly, with 6 pre-existing
failures fully root-caused. Thank you — I welcome your questions."

=== [2] 10-MINUTE DETAILED ACADEMIC DEFENSE SCRIPT (~1,350 words) ===

"Respected Chair, examiners, and faculty members, welcome to the technical
presentation of ANPR-CampusGate.

[SLIDES 1-4: CONTEXT & PROBLEM]
Indian university campuses handle heterogeneous traffic — two-wheelers, sedans,
heavy delivery trucks, and campus buses — under harsh gate lighting and camera
skew. Standard license plate detectors fail in three ways: they re-log the same
slow car repeatedly, corrupt characters like DL7CD5017 when blurry, and run far
too slowly (~1 FPS) on non-GPU gate PCs. ANPR-CampusGate solves all three while
connecting sightings across multiple gates into vehicle movement trajectories.

[SLIDES 5-7: ARCHITECTURE & FLOW — FIGURES 01 & 02]
Tracing a single frame through run_pipeline.py: FrameCapture pulls frames from
RTSP streams or queued MP4 files (`frame_skip=1`, `stall_timeout=10s`). Vehicle
YOLO (`yolov8n.onnx` at 480px) locates cars, motorcycles, buses, and trucks
above 0.50 confidence. ByteTrack maintains continuous `track_id` values with
`lost_track_timeout=60` frames. Any vehicle track not yet in `stored_tracks`
runs `MotionFilter` (`>=3px` movement) and enters Plate YOLO
(`best_finetuned.onnx` at 320px, `conf=0.28`, min size `36x12px`). To bound CPU
spikes in crowded scenes, at most 2 heavy tracks per file frame (or 1 live) run
full OCR per frame in round-robin priority.

Each cropped plate is resized to `64px` height, enhanced via adaptive gamma LUT
and CLAHE (`sr_threshold_px=0` bypasses heavy super-resolution on CPU), and read
by `RapidOCR` (DBNet + SVTR ONNX, angle classifier off). Next,
`ocr_postprocessor` enforces Indian RTO regex and position-specific confusion
repairs (`0/O`, `1/I`, `8/B`, `5/S`, `2/Z`), triggering immediate pre-store
blacklist alerts at `>=0.70` confidence while buffering candidates inside
`OCRFusion` (`window=5`). Once 3 uncontested reads agree at `>=0.82` (or 2 at
`>=0.60`), `DuplicateFilter` checks a 30-second sliding window with Levenshtein
distance `<= 2`, `DirectionDetector` classifies IN vs OUT using calibrated gate
vectors and virtual lines, and `ALPRDatabase.insert_event()` commits the row.

[SLIDES 8-13: DATASET & MODEL TRAINING FORENSICS — FIGURES 04, 05, 06]
Because academic rigor demands verifying claims against artifacts, we performed
a complete repository audit. First, training configurations documented 25,674
images, but `dataset_validation_report.json` and `train-3/train.log` prove the
verified disk dataset had 20,580 train images (split by Ultralytics into 18,578
train + 1,973 val) and 978 test images = 21,529–21,558 images; '4,116' was an
erroneous 20% addition on top of 20,580. On this clean split, YOLOv8s `train-3`
converged over 94 epochs in 6.13 hours on an RTX 2000 Ada to `0.9832` peak
mAP50 (`0.6943` mAP50-95 at epoch 74) and `0.6918` fitness on the 978-image test
set.

Second, deployed `best_finetuned.onnx` (12.88 MB) is a lighter 3.14M-parameter
YOLOv8n trained on Kaggle T4 (49 epochs, `0.9783` peak val mAP50) initialized
from COCO `yolov8n.pt` rather than fine-tuned from YOLOv8s `best.pt`. Because the
`.ipynb` notebook is unexecuted and the 833-image zip is outside the repo, we
explicitly flag that dataset split as unverified locally. Third, our audit
caught and quarantined a legacy YOLOv8m run (`train/`) whose `mAP50=0.9949` was
inflated by `val == train` leakage inside `dataset_converter.py`. On OCR, we
verified 3,767 cropped plate images in `OCR_data` (1,075 unique plates; heavy
Maharashtra 37.9% and Delhi 13.9% skew) used to train PARSeq-Tiny (`val_loss =
0.0955` at Epoch 8).

[SLIDES 14-22: PERSISTENCE, ORCHESTRATION & FRONTEND — FIGURE 03]
On storage [Figure 03], active persistence runs on SQLite WAL mode across
`vehicle_events` (22 columns) and `vehicle_profiles` (25 columns), synced via
nested `SAVEPOINT` without brittle SQL foreign keys. Note that an unused
6-table normalized schema exists in `postgres_schema.py` as future PostgreSQL
scaffolding. Multi-camera management separates sequential MP4 upload queuing
from parallel RTSP worker threads synchronized around shared ONNX inference via
`threading.RLock()`. Operators interact via a Next.js 15 console wired through
41 FastAPI REST/MJPEG endpoints, including a Leaflet multi-gate campus map and
natural-language trajectory search.

[SLIDES 23-28: BENCHMARKS, TESTING & HONEST LIMITATIONS — FIGURES 07 & 08]
Our latency benchmarks [Figure 07] prove practical x86 CPU feasibility: cutting
plate inference from 101.6 ms to 13.4 ms and OCR from 556.5 ms to 23.6 ms
yielded 19.18 FPS across the full 1,500-frame benchmark (`frame_skip=1`, 6/6
vehicles detected). Our 758-test pytest suite [Figure 08] verifies core
modules cleanly, with all 6 failing tests cleanly explained by pre-existing test
drift (`test_parseq_dataset`, `test_super_resolution`, and Streamlit 1.67.0
`AppTest` element changes). Finally, we transparently disclose three remaining
gaps as immediate next validation steps: a labeled OCR CER benchmark across
`OCR_data`, sustained 5-minute dual-camera campus RTSP stress testing, and
on-device NVIDIA Jetson TensorRT benchmarking. Thank you."

SECTION C - 35 TECHNICAL VIVA QUESTIONS & EVIDENTIARY MODEL ANSWERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q01: What is your project's actual end-to-end accuracy, and can you quote
     0.9832 mAP50 as your system accuracy?
A01: No — `0.9832` is strictly YOLOv8s `train-3` plate bounding-box localization
     `mAP50` on its 1,973-image validation split (`0.6943 mAP50-95` at Ep 74).
     End-to-end ALPR accuracy depends on detection recall x ByteTrack continuity
     x OCR string accuracy x fusion voting. We transparently report that plate-
     level CER / exact match on `OCR_data` is unbenchmarked locally, while our
     full 1,500-frame video benchmark caught 6/6 vehicles at `frame_skip=1`.

Q02: Why do your project configs say 25,674 training images when your audit
     reports ~21,529-21,558?
A02: `dataset_validation_report.json` states `"Split 'val' not found"` with
     `20,580 train + 978 test = 21,558`. `train.log` shows Ultralytics carved
     `1,973 val` out of `20,580` (`18,578 train + 1,973 val = 20,551`). The
     `4,116` val figure in `config.json` equals `0.20 * 20,580`, erroneously
     added on top of `20,580` instead of partitioned from it.

Q03: Was `best_finetuned.onnx` fine-tuned by resuming weights from `best.pt`?
A03: No. Forensic `torch.load` inspection proved `best.pt` is 11.14M-param
     YOLOv8s, whereas `best_finetuned.pt` is 3.14M-param YOLOv8n trained from
     COCO `yolov8n.pt` on Kaggle T4 (49 epochs, `mAP50=0.9697/0.9783`). It was
     chosen as the active detector to minimize CPU latency (`13.4 ms` vs heavy
     `YOLOv8s`).

Q04: How do you prove legacy run `train/` had data leakage?
A04: Two independent facts: (1) `training/dataset_converter.py` (L58-62) wrote
     identical folder paths to both `train:` and `val:` keys; (2) in
     `train/results.csv`, validation box loss (`0.4154`) finished LOWER than
     training box loss (`0.4469`), yielding an unnatural `0.9949` mAP50.

Q05: Why can't we deploy your fine-tuned PARSeq OCR ONNX model on CPU right now?
A05: When executing `models/ocr/parseq_official_tiny.onnx` under CPU `onnxruntime`,
     the transformer decoder hits `NotImplemented: Where(16)`. Thus CPU runs
     use `RapidOCR`, reserving `parseq_official_tiny.engine` for TensorRT 10.16
     NVIDIA Jetson/GPU hosts.

Q06: Explain how you achieved a 15x speedup on CPU (1.25 FPS -> 19.18 FPS)
     without losing detections.
A06: Four concrete changes: (1) Custom `_onnx_yolo.py` ORT inference + lightweight
     YOLOv8n (`320px` plate imgsz) cut plate det from 101.6 ms to 13.4 ms;
     (2) RapidOCR `det_limit_type="max"`, `det_limit_side_len=320`, and
     `use_cls=false` slashed OCR from 556.5 ms to 23.6 ms; (3) `sr_threshold_px=0`
     bypassed CPU super-resolution; (4) `stored_tracks.add(tid)` eliminated
     repeat OCR once a vehicle track reached consensus.

Q07: Why is `frame_skip=1` slower in wall time (`78.2s` vs `43.8s`) than
     `frame_skip=2`, yet preferred in `config.yaml`?
A07: In our ablation on 1,500-frame `test_video.mp4`, `frame_skip=2` halved
     processed frames but doubled inter-frame bbox motion, breaking ByteTrack
     IoU matching and dropping Track 1 (5/6 vehicles caught vs 6/6 with
     `frame_skip=1`). `frame_skip=1` also achieved higher per-frame throughput
     (19.18 vs 17.12 FPS) and zero dropped vehicles.

Q08: How does pre-store blacklist alerting work, and why not check after DB save?
A08: Waiting for `OCRFusion` (3 agreeing frames) plus DB insert delays alerts by
     up to 25 frames. Instead, `run_pipeline.py` queries `BlacklistManager`
     immediately after any validated single-frame read meets `conf >= 0.70`,
     firing alerts +187.9 ms to +1,127.0 ms earlier. A flag prevents re-firing
     when Step 13 commits the event.

Q09: What happens if a plate is misread slightly on two consecutive frames, say
     `DL7CD5017` vs `DD77D5017`?
A09: First, `ocr_postprocessor.py` applies Indian RTO slot logic (forcing letters
     in positions 0-1 and digits in 2-3/tail). Second, `DuplicateFilter` uses a
     30-second window and merges same-length strings within `Levenshtein <= 2`
     (explicitly raised from 1 to 2 for this exact failure case), preventing
     duplicate DB events.

Q10: What is the tradeoff of `max_edit_distance: 2` in `DuplicateFilter`?
A10: As documented in `config/config.yaml`, two genuinely distinct vehicles with
     near-sequential fleet plates (e.g., `MH12AB1001` and `MH12AB1002`, edit
     distance 1-2, same length) passing the exact same gate within 30 seconds
     would be suppressed as duplicates. It is ideal for moderate campus flow
     but should drop back to `<= 1` or rely on distinct `track_id` separation at
     high-throughput toll gates.

Q11: Why does `DuplicateFilter` require `len(a) == len(b)` before Levenshtein?
A11: Without equal-length guarding, a truncated 7-char partial read (`MH12AB1`)
     compared against an unrelated 9-char read (`MH12AB123`) already consumes 2
     edits purely in length difference, creating false-positive merges.

Q12: Walk through `OCRFusion`'s consensus vs EOF flush rules.
A12: `OCRFusion` maintains a 5-frame deque per track. Early exit commits as soon
     as 3 uncontested reads agree at weighted confidence `>= 0.82` (or `min_exact_votes=2`
     with `min_confidence >= 0.60`). When a video clip ends, `flush_all()` finalizes
     remaining unstored valid tracks with relaxed `>= 0.9 * min_confidence = 0.54`.

Q13: Why are there no SQL `FOREIGN KEY` constraints between `vehicle_events`
     and `vehicle_profiles`?
A13: Joining logically via uppercase `plate_number` lets session clears
     (`POST /api/events/clear`) or legacy imports add/delete event batches
     without foreign-key cascade lockups. Sync uses a nested SQLAlchemy
     `SAVEPOINT` inside `insert_event()` so profile JSON errors never roll back
     the captured sighting event.

Q14: What is `src/database/postgres_schema.py` doing if active ORM only uses
     2 tables?
A14: Audit trace proved `postgres_schema.py` (6 normalized tables + 1
     materialized view `daily_traffic_stats`) is completely unreferenced by
     active runtime code. Even under PostgreSQL (`DATABASE_URL`), SQLAlchemy
     binds to `models.py` (`vehicle_events` and `vehicle_profiles`).

Q15: Why did `evaluation_report.json` save `fitness: 0.691836` while `precision`,
     `recall`, and `mAP50` were empty?
A15: `training/evaluate.py` had a key-lookup mismatch extracting Ultralytics
     metrics dictionary fields after `model.val(split='test')` ran across all
     978 test images (`evaluate.log` verified the run, checkpoint fitness is
     `0.1*mAP50 + 0.9*mAP50-95`).

Q16: Explain how `VehicleTracker` keeps IDs during momentary occlusion, and what
     happens if `supervision` fails.
A16: `supervision.ByteTrack` uses a 7-state Kalman filter prediction plus
     two-stage Hungarian IoU assignment matching high-conf then low-conf boxes,
     keeping occluded tracks alive up to `lost_track_timeout = 60` processed
     frames (`min_matching_thresh = 0.5`). If unavailable, `VehicleTracker`
     falls back transparently to greedy frame-to-frame IoU matching.

Q17: How does `DirectionDetector` distinguish `IN`, `OUT`, and `UNKNOWN`?
A17: It tracks centroid histories `[cx, cy]` over `15` frames (`min_confirm_frames=3`,
     `min_displacement_px=25.0`), checking dot-product alignment within
     `cone_angle_deg=55.0` against calibrated `in_vector`/`out_vector` vectors
     and `virtual_line` intersections per camera (`ch01`/`ch02`). Stationary cars
     yield `UNKNOWN`.

Q18: Why cap `max_heavy_tracks_per_frame` at `2` (files) / `1` (live)?
A18: Heavy plate-YOLO + RapidOCR cost scales linearly (~20-40 ms/vehicle on CPU).
     In a live 30 FPS feed, inspecting 4 cars in 1 frame overflows 33 ms frame
     interval, causing `FrameCapture` to drop frames, which jumps box positions
     and fragments ByteTrack IDs! Round-robin budget gating keeps frame pacing
     smooth while un-stored cars wait 1-2 frames for OCR.

Q19: Why set `enhancment.sr_threshold_px = 0` when `preprocessing.target_height_px = 64`?
A19: RealESRGAN weights (`RealESRGAN_x4plus.pth`) are absent from disk, and
     OpenCV cubic/SR fallback adds slow per-crop overhead before DBNet. Setting
     `target_height_px = 64` handles fast pre-CLAHE upscaling directly at
     minimal cost.

Q20: How does `VehicleColorDetector` avoid getting fooled by sky reflections or
     windshields?
A20: Config `vehicle_color` sets `region_top = 0.35`, skipping the top 35% of
     the bounding box (roof/windshield glare), sampling hood/body HSV channels,
     thresholding `min_saturation=55` / `min_value=50`, and separating white/
     silver/gray/black cleanly by median brightness percentile.

Q21: How does `CameraManager` prevent thread contention between concurrent
     cameras and uploaded videos?
A21: Uploads run strictly sequentially via a single FIFO background queue
     (`data/processing_status.json`), whereas live multi-camera RTSP capture
     threads run in parallel and share ONNX model inference safely through a
     re-entrant `threading.RLock()`.

Q22: How do live MJPEG streams avoid half-written corrupt JPEGs on Windows?
A22: `LiveFramePublisher` and status writers write bytes to a temporary file
     via `tempfile.mkstemp()` in the same directory and execute atomic
     `os.replace()`, which is atomic on Windows NTFS metadata updates.

Q23: Why is `SQLite WAL mode` critical here?
A23: Default SQLite rollback journaling blocks concurrent readers during writes.
     Enabling `PRAGMA journal_mode=WAL`, `synchronous=NORMAL`, and a 30-second
     busy timeout allows Next.js FastAPI polling (`GET /api/events`, `/api/stats`)
     to run concurrently with active video inserts without `database is locked` errors.

Q24: Explain the React hydration error you fixed in Next.js 15.
A24: Browser form/autofill extensions inject attributes like `fdprocessedid`
     into `<button>` and `<input>` elements before React hydrates, plus client-
     side state differences triggered hydration mismatch warnings. Guarding
     client-only rendering and normalizing button markup eliminated console errors.

Q25: Why are there 6 failures across your 758-test pytest suite, and do they
     indicate broken production logic?
A25: None of the 6 fail on core runtime paths: 3 unit failures (`test_parseq_dataset`,
     `test_super_resolution`) reflect outdated test mocks vs refactored APIs;
     3 Streamlit `dashboard_v2` failures stem from Streamlit `1.67.0` changing
     `at.get("imgs")` -> `at.get("image")` and hardcoded `Sept 12` test dates
     falling outside `"Last 7 days"`.

Q26: Why did `test_trajectory_api` / `TestClient` hang in the automated sandbox
     but pass on host execution?
A26: The isolated execution sandbox blocks loopback sockets required by FastAPI
     `TestClient` / ASGI lifespan and Streamlit `AppTest` threads. Running pure
     DB sub-suites (`-k "TestSchemaMigration or TestInsertAndRead"`) in-sandbox
     passed 15/15 in 1.97s, and host execution passed 25 ASGI tests cleanly.

Q27: Compare `YOLOv8` loss functions — why use CIoU + DFL instead of plain L1/L2?
A27: Plain L2 misses scale invariance and aspect ratio alignment. `CIoU`
     simultaneously optimizes IoU overlap, normalized center distance, and plate
     aspect ratio `v`, while Distribution Focal Loss (`DFL`) models box edge
     coordinates as probability distributions across 16 discrete bins for tight
     localization around narrow rectangular license plates.

Q28: Why does `RapidOCR` keep `use_det=true` on already-cropped plates instead
     of passing straight to recognition?
A28: Many Indian two-wheeler and commercial plates have **two-line** layouts
     (`MH 12` over `AB 1234`). Keeping DBNet `use_det=true` (with `det_border_pad=16`
     and `det_limit_side_len=320`) isolates both text lines reliably so the
     adapter can sort top-to-bottom and concatenate them in reading order.

Q29: How does `OCR_data` class/state distribution affect model generalization?
A29: Direct file audit showed `MH` (37.9%) and `DL` (13.9%) dominate over half
     the 3,767 images, while Northern/Eastern states tested in demo videos (`UP`,
     `BR`) are sparsely represented (<1.5%). Expanding regional balanced plate
     crops is a primary recommendation.

Q30: Explain how multi-gate vehicle trajectory speed and distances are computed.
A30: `VehicleProfile` appends `{camera_id, timestamp, direction, latitude,
     longitude}` into `trajectory_history` (up to 500 entries). `TrajectoryEngine`
     calculates Haversine surface distance between consecutive gate GPS coordinates
     and divides by timestamp delta to build campus transition graphs and Leaflet maps.

Q31: What security vulnerability exists in the current FastAPI backend?
A31: All 41 REST endpoints — including destructive routes like `POST /api/events/clear`,
     `POST/DELETE /api/blacklist`, and camera mutations — currently lack JWT/API-key
     authentication or role-based access control. In deployment, they must sit
     behind campus VPN/VLAN restrictions or an authenticating reverse proxy.

Q32: How do `whitelist.yaml` and `blacklist.yaml` stay in sync during long
     camera runs without restarting the server?
A32: Both managers cache entries alongside file modification timestamps via
     `os.path.getmtime()` and transparently reload from disk whenever an API
     mutation or admin edit updates the YAML file.

Q33: Why didn't you evaluate live dual-camera RTSP latency in this audit?
A33: Physical dual campus RTSP feeds require active campus network connectivity
     for >= 5 minutes with simultaneous live vehicles. Because this environment
     ran offline/sandboxed with prerecorded gate clips, we documented dual-RTSP
     stress testing explicitly as an unverified deployment step.

Q34: If asked to migrate tomorrow to an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano at a physical
     gate, what changes in `config/config.yaml`?
A34: Point `vehicle_model_path` to `models/vehicle_detector/yolov8n.engine`,
     `plate_model_path` to TensorRT `best.engine` / exported `best_finetuned.engine`,
     switch `ocr.backend` to `"parseq_tensorrt"` (`parseq_official_tiny.engine`),
     optionally supply `RealESRGAN_x4plus.pth` with `sr_threshold_px: 80`, and
     run dual-RTSP 5-min verification to calibrate `live_max_heavy_tracks_per_frame`.

Q35: What single upgrade would deliver the highest scientific credibility right now?
A35: Implementing an automated ground-truth OCR & end-to-end gate accuracy
     benchmark script across stratified `OCR_data` + labeled campus CCTV clips
     to report verified Character Error Rate (CER), exact plate accuracy, and
     Confusion Matrix metrics alongside existing YOLO mAP scores.
